# Session 2: Benchmarking Exercise

**This is not graded on whether you can do it.** It is graded on whether you turn
something in and describe honestly what happened. Its purpose is to show us where the
class is starting from, and to show you where we are going.

## The setup

You work as an analyst. Your manager is smart, cares about the answer, and does not
write code. They have asked you three questions. They do not care what tools you use.
They want a short written answer, backed by a figure, that they could forward to someone
else without embarrassment.

## The questions

Pick **one**. Do not attempt all three.

1. **Which countries' CO2 emissions are rising fastest, and which are falling?**
   Over the last thirty years or so. Your manager wants to know who is actually
   changing direction, not just who is biggest.

2. **Which industries contribute most to global emissions?**
   Not which countries - which *sectors*. Your manager has seen plenty of
   emissions-by-country charts and finds them unactionable.

3. **Is Arctic sea ice actually declining, and by how much?**
   Your manager has heard the claim and the pushback and wants to see the record.

## What to hand in

A public GitHub repository containing:

- A notebook or script that produces your answer
- At least one figure
- A `README.md` written for your manager: what you found, how confident you are, what
  you would want to check if you had more time
- A commit history that shows how you worked

## Constraints

- **Work individually** for this one.
- Use whatever tools, libraries, websites, or assistance you like. There is no restriction
  and nothing is off-limits. Tell us in the README what you used.
- Timebox it. If you are stuck after a couple of hours, hand in what you have plus a
  written account of where you got stuck. That account is genuinely as useful to us as a
  finished answer.

## Finding data

Part of the exercise. If you cannot find suitable data, say so in the README and describe
what you looked for. Knowing that a question is hard to source is itself a finding.

## What we are looking for

Nothing here is a trick. We want to see:

- Whether you can get from a vague question to a specific, answerable one
- Whether your figure communicates without a paragraph of explanation
- Whether you say what you are unsure about
- Whether the repository is something you would be willing to show someone

By the end of the semester this should feel routine. Right now it may not, and that is
the point of measuring it today.

## A note on how you will feel about this later

Keep this repository. We will come back to it. Several of the things that make this hard
today - finding the data, getting it parsed correctly, knowing whether your numbers are
right - are exactly what the next four modules are about.
